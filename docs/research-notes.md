# Research Notes

## Trust and trader-location research

There is no high-quality dataset that directly ranks which nationality of individual social-media Forex traders people trust most.

Useful proxies measure different things and should not be conflated:
- Financial-services trust surveys measure trust in institutions/services, not necessarily individual social-media traders.
- FX turnover measures market importance, not personal credibility.
- Retail participation measures activity, not trust.
- Regulatory reputation can inform a credibility framework, but does not prove that an individual trader is trustworthy.

## Project implication

Do not automatically assign a nationality to a fictional trader because it supposedly creates more trust. A professional persona should use the user's real identity/location or be clearly disclosed as a fictional/brand persona.

Credibility should instead come from verifiable information, transparent methodology, market-data sources, consistent risk disclosure, clear separation of fact and opinion, honest credentials and consistent publishing.

## Data handling

For current or historical market prices, retrieve and record the actual source and timestamp when accuracy matters.

Never manufacture historical candles, trade fills, account balances, profit statements, broker statements, credentials, employment history, regulatory licenses, partnerships or performance records.
